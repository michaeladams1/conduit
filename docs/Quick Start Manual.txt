                                  Engine Startup Procedures
Follow these steps in sequence to ensure a safe and successful pump start.
   1. Prepare the area for pump operations.
          a. Alert all personnel in the immediate vicinity that pumping operations are about to begin.
          b. Ensure you are wearing all required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), including safety
              glasses, a hard hat, and steel-toed boots.
          c. Confirm that the pumpage fluid is available at the suction inlet.
          d. Open the priming isolation valve (if equipped).
   2. Start the engine.
          a. Turn the key to the START position. Once the engine ignites and is running steadily, release the
              key to the ON position.
          b. Gradually increase the throttle to half speed.
   3. Monitor the pump.
          a. While at half speed, perform a "walk-around" inspection:
                   i. Listen for abnormal noises and check for excessive vibrations or fluid leaks. Correct any
                       issues immediately before proceeding.
                  ii. Verify that all control panel gauges and displays are functioning and showing normal
                       operating parameters.
   4. Adjust performance as needed.
          a. Adjust the engine speed to the desired operational level once the system has stabilized.
   5. Continuously monitor the bearing housing and stuffing box for signs of overheating
          a. Use the back of your hand (never the palm) to check the temperature of these components. If the
              housing is too hot to maintain contact, this may indicate binding internal parts or a lubrication
              failure.
          b. If the pump is moving high-temperature liquids, use a handheld IR temperature sensor for an
              accurate and safe reading.

                                    Operational Monitoring
After the engine has started and the pump has primed, perform a walk-around inspection to confirm the
following conditions:

   •   Engine Performance
          o The engine should produce a consistent, smooth sound without rhythmic knocking, surging, or
              metallic clicking.
          o Exhaust should be clear. Persistent black, white, or blue smoke indicates a combustion or fluid
              leak issue.
   •   Vacuum and Priming
          o Verify that the vacuum reading is within the acceptable range of 10 to 28 inHg.
                  ▪ Note: A reading below 10 inHg may indicate a suction leak, while a reading near 30
                      inHg may indicate a plugged suction line.




   •   Vital Gauges
           o Ensure the engine oil pressure is within the manufacturer's specified range for the current RPM.
           o Monitor the water temperature gauge to ensure the engine is operating within its optimal thermal
              range (typically 180°F to 205°F).
   •   Fluid Movement
           o Confirm that water is moving through the pump by checking the discharge pressure gauge or
              visually inspecting the discharge point.
           o Do not continue operation if fluid is not moving, as this can lead to overheating of the
              mechanical seal and internal components.

                              Engine Shutdown Procedures
To ensure the engine and pump components cool evenly, always follow this controlled shutdown sequence.
   1. Gradually reduce the engine speed to its lowest idle setting.
         a. If the unit is equipped with a manual clutch, disengage the pump from the engine drive.
   2. Allow the engine to idle for approximately 5 minutes before turning it off.
         a. This "soak period" allows the coolant and oil to circulate, lowering the temperature of the
              turbocharger and internal components.
         b. Skipping this step can lead to oil carbonization and premature bearing failure.
   3. Once the cool-down period is complete, rotate the ignition switch from the RUN position to the OFF
      position.
         a. Remove the key to prevent unauthorized starting or battery drain.

               Starting the Engine Using the Autostart Controller
When equipped, the Auto Start controller automatically manages engine cycles using external sensors. This
system is compatible with standard Mechanical Float Switches (normally open, weighted) or Submersible
Liquid Level Transmitters (4-20mA).
Sensor Configuration
Receptacles for these sensors are typically located on the bottom of the control panel.
   • Use a pair of weighted floats for "pump down" operations. We recommend color-coding them for
       clarity:
           o YELLOW (High Float): Set at the high-water mark; provides the START signal.
           o RED (Low Float): Set at the low-water mark; provides the STOP signal.
   • A submersible transmitter can be connected to the dedicated 4-20mA receptacle if precise level
       monitoring is preferred.




System Activation
DANGER: When in AUTO mode, the engine will start WITHOUT WARNING once the water reaches the
high-level mark. Ensure all personnel are clear of the unit and all guards are secure before leaving the site.

    1. Rotate the ignition key to the AUTO position.
    2. The system will perform a self-test and display the
       start-up screen.
    3. Power Save Mode: To conserve battery, the display
       will go dormant after approximately 120 seconds. The
       system remains fully functional while the screen is
       off; a red LED will blink once every 10 seconds to
       indicate "Standby" status.

Operational Test Procedure
To verify the Auto Start logic is functioning correctly, perform the following test:
   1. Set the control panel Start/Stop Input to “Float(s)”.
   2. Rotate key to AUTO. Red standby LED illuminates.
   3. Simulate LOW.
          a. Use a jumper wire to short Pins 2 & 3 of the LOW-LEVEL FLOAT receptacle.
   4. Simulate HIGH.
          a. Use a jumper wire to short Pins 1 & 4 of the HIGH-LEVEL FLOAT receptacle.




   5. The display will wake up and show: “Engine Start in X:XX”.




   6. The engine will start after the programmed delay (default is 10 seconds).
To stop the engine: Remove the float plugs or rotate the ignition key to OFF.
