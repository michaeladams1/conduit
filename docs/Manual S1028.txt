                                               Safety
General Precautions

Below is a list of general precautions that should be followed with centrifugal pumps and their operation.
These precautions will ensure the longevity of the pump, as well as the safety of the operator and other
staff.

  1. Only pump approved fluids with this pump. If you are uncertain as to solids size, volatility, or any
     other concern with the liquid, consult the factory.
  2. Do not allow pump, piping or other components to freeze (except refrigeration pumps). Freezing
     can damage pump components, as well as other parts of the system.
  3. Wear proper safety gear (safety glasses, visibility vest, hearing protection, steel-toed footwear,
     etc.) when operating the pump.
  4. Wear proper safety gear when servicing the pump.
  5. Periodically inspect the pump system, including the pump.
  6. Conduct routine maintenance as required.
  7. Remember that pumps are rotating equipment that can impart great force. Be sure to keep
     clothing, hair, etc., away from rotating shafts. Guards and shields should be used wherever and
     whenever practical.
  8. Flush pump thoroughly with clean water, before opening the pump and after removing
     components, flush them thoroughly with clean water.
  9. Follow applicable health and safety protocols.
 10. Use lifting and moving equipment in good repair, with the proper capacity, to prevent injuries.

Servicing Pump

Before attempting to open or service the pump,
  1. Power down and lock out drive
  2. Check temperature of pump before opening
  3. Allow pump to cool down if too hot to handle
  4. Close suction and discharge valves/gates and isolate the pump
  5. Drain pump

Operation


         Use for intended purpose: Ensure that the corrosiveness and abrasiveness of the
         liquid, as well as the solids-handling size of the pump, match what you are pumping.
         Pump shoulld not be used to pump volatile or flammable liquids unless explicitly
         specified to do so on application and siting from the factory.



         Before start-up: Follow the Start-up Checklist in this manual. After initial placement,
         ensure that pump and all pipe/valve fittings/connections are tight, properly supported,
         and secured.



         Guards: Do not operate pump without proper guards in place over rotating parts.
         Unguarded rotating parts can catch tools, clothing, and limbs, causing injury.



                                               page 9 of 46
                                         CORNELL PUMP COMPANY


         Discharges: Ensure discharge valve is operable. Do not operate pump without an
         open discharge valve, except in cases of proper dry priming.


         Gauges and fittings: Do not remove gauges, fittings, plugs etc. from pump without a
         proper replacement. Do not remove plugs, fittings, etc. in an instance where the pump
         has overheated.



         Cavitation: Avoid operating the pump at cavitation as it will shorten the life of the pump
         and could cause a pump failure.



         Internal Combustion Engines: Do not operate engines in enclosed space without
         venting exhaust fumes to the outside. Do not operate engines in explosive
         atmospheres. Be certain to secure fuel, as it can present an explosion hazard. Always
         use the proper fuel for the engine. DO NOT refill/refuel with a hot or running engine.


Receiving Pump
Pump should be inspected as soon as it is received. Check pump thoroughly for shipping damage or
missing components, ensuring that it is in good working order. If a shipping claim needs to be filed, it
should be done as soon as possible.

Storing pumps
Pumps are packaged to arrive safely through normal shipping, and to be stored for up to three months
before use. The pump should be stored in dry location, covered from elements (building or tarping), and
flange protectors should be left on to discourage entrance by fluids or animals into the pump.

If storing for a longer period, bearing oil should be winterized, and machined surfaces protected with a
rust inhibitor. Shaft should be rotated every two months to decrease chance for oxidation and binding.
Motors, couplings, and guards should be maintained per manufacturers' operation and maintenance
handbooks. Long term storage should be in a dry, covered location.

Handling
Pumps are heavy. Every precaution should be taken for safe moving and deploying. Properly rated lifting
devices should be used to move pumps—never use a worn, frayed, or under-capacity strap.

                                 Start-up Checklist

BEFORE THE START-UP OF ANY PUMP, A CAREFUL CHECK MUST BE MADE TO ENSURE THAT ALL IS IN ORDER:

1. Reread all instructions and check for compliance on each point.

2. Make sure that
     a. All fasteners and gasketed joints are properly tightened.
     b. Belts and couplings are properly adjusted, shafts aligned, and guards are in place.
     c. All thrust blocks and supports are adequate.
     d. The pump and/or baseplate is bolted securely to a solid foundation.
     e. There are no piping loads on the pump casing, support suction, or discharge piping.
      f. Piping is clean and free of debris and obstructions.
     g. Gaskets are in place and all joints are secure.
     h. All electrical connections and electrical equipment are installed by a
         qualified and licensed electrical contractor.
      i. The pump rotates freely by hand; then check the pump rotational
         direction with very short on/off power pulses on the starter switch.
      j. Pump is not run dry unless it is equipped with RunDryTM option.
     k. For self-priming (wet prime) pumps only; fill pump casing with liquid completely.


             CAUTION: Pumps with mechanical seal must not run dry unless equipped with
             run-dry feature.


             Some pumps are equipped with threaded shafts (e.g. food, slurry, self-priming,
             and some solid handling pumps). Improper rotation will cause failure and costly
             repairs. If you are unsure if your pump has a threaded shaft, contact the factory.


3. Valves
   Check the valves for proper position. If the system has a discharge gate valve, start with the valve
   closed. The speed of opening depends upon the size and length of your discharge pipe and
   capacity of the pump. The valve should not be more than 1/4 open until the line is filled. The
   purpose of this procedure is to reduce the possibility of a water hammer or shock if filling is too
   rapid.

4. For Stuffing Box with Packing
   When first starting the pump, there should be leakage. If the packing is too loose, air will suck in
   around the packing, and the pump will not prime. After the pump is primed, back off on packing
   gland nuts to free leakage. Several minutes later, gradually and evenly tighten packing gland nuts
   until leakage is reduced to a trickle. Do not attempt to shut off all the leakage - packing pumps must
   leak to lubricate the packing.

   For Stuffing Box with Mechanical Seal
   Read and comply with all seal instructions within the manual. Occasionally, new installations will
   leak very small amounts for a short time. These leaks must be inspected daily. If leakage doesn't
   reduce to almost zero, then assembly should be inspected for proper mechanical seal installation.

5. Instructions for Pumps with Balance Lines and Wear Rings (if present)
   Wear rings and balance lines are vital for a successful pump operation. They perform two important
   functions. First, they reduce the pressure at the stuffing box. Second, they reduce axial thrust

                                            page 14 of 46
                                        CORNELL PUMP COMPANY

   loads. Wear rings should be replaced if the clearance has increased to about .030" per side.
   Balance lines should be kept free of obstructions and they should be replaced if they are pinched,
   bent, or corroded.

6. Motors
   Check the power supply voltage, amperage, temperature, and RPM with the motor nameplate.
   Review item 2(i) with respect to rotation.


              Large motors must not be started and stopped more than five times per hour.


              CAUTION: Whether placed inside or outside, the motor should be mounted on a
              base which is four to six inches higher than the surrounding floor level.


7. Bearing Frames
   In general, the considerations for a bearing frame are the same for that of electric motors.

   NOTE: If a frame is oil lubed (denoted by ‘K’ on serial number plate and sight gauge on the side of
   the frame), put appropriate oil in per lubrication instructions. Make sure support systems are in
   place and working, such as special lubricants, seal water, etc.

   If the pump is used in winter, provisions must be made for protection of the pump and piping from
   freezing. Add a heater if necessary. If the pump is not used in the winter, the volute should be
   drained to prevent damage.


              Never operate electric motors or pump equipment without all protective covers,
              screens and guards properly in place. Before disconnecting any electrical
              wiring, shut off the main switch and lock it out.


8. Check to make sure the suction screens are in place. A suction screen or strainer should have a
   free opening at least three times the area of the suction pipe.

9. Start the driver. If pump is primed or filled with liquid, the pumping will start immediately. For self-
   priming (wet prime) pumps, be sure casing is full of liquid prior to start-up.

   Centrifugal Pump Priming
   A centrifugal pump is primed when all the internal passages of the pump are filled with the liquid to
   be pumped. Do not operate any pump without being properly primed unless it has been specifically
   designed for such operation (such as wet prime pumps). NOTE: for wet prime pumps only; the
   discharge pipe must let air escape from the system while the pump is priming.

   When the pump is primed and the unit is started, the pumpage will start to flow immediately. If it
   does not, recheck the system for complete prime and possible air leaks. Correct the deficiencies
   and restart.


       CAUTION: If the pumpage does not start immediately, no amount of additional
       pumping will solve the problem.

       Do not allow compressed air to pressurize the pump or vent-off compressed air
       through the pump as this may damage pump or cause serious personal injury.

Disassembly (Refer to the parts page for part names)

  1. Volute Removal
     If the pump is equipped with a run dry reservoir, drain the reservoir, disconnect the hoses from the
     backplate, and remove the reservoir from the pump. Remove all capscrews holding the volute to
     the backplate. Insert two of these capscrews into tapped holes in the backplate. Tighten the
     capscrews to jack the volute free of the backplate, exposing the impeller. Remove the volute.

               Make sure the volute is supported so that it doesn't fall onto the impeller.


  2. Impeller Removal
     Impeller lockscrews are always right hand socket head capscrews. Lockscrews are installed with
     Loctite 262, which may inhibit removal. Remove the impeller lockscrew by breaker bar, not by
     impact wrench. Make sure to apply steady and even torque to break lockscrew loose. Allen head
     tooling to remove the lockscrew should be in excellent condition. If breaker bar will not loosen
     impeller lockscrew, apply heat to the lockscrew for 2-4 minutes, loosening the Loctite. Discard the
     lockscrew. Remove the impeller washer.
               Serious burn injury is possible when applying heat. Do not exceed 400° F. Allow
               the lockscrew to cool before removing it.


     Space wedges in pairs 180° apart along the vanes between the impeller and backplate. Tap
     opposed wedges at the same time to force off the impeller. Use extreme care to avoid damage to
     impeller, shaft and bearings. Completely remove the impeller and impeller key.

               Be sure the wedges are placed along the impeller vanes or impeller damage will
               occur.


     An alternate method is shown here. Install a
     capscrew or bolt in the shaft thread to protect the
     threads. Attach an impeller puller to the backplate
     and snug up the nuts on the rods so that the
     puller is against the impeller. Remove the
     capscrews between bracket and backplate.
     Tighten the center rod to jack the backplate and
     impeller off the shaft. The seal will also come off.

  3. Seal Removal
     Remove the mechanical seal spring.
     Lubricate the sleeve and work off the
     rotating portion of the seal, being careful to
     avoid damage to the sealing face. The
     rubber bellows will be firmly attached to the
     sleeve and will require considerable
     pressure to remove it. The stationary seat
     will remain in the backplate.

  4. Backplate Removal
     Unbolt and remove the backplate from the pump bracket.

               If the seal stationary seat is to be reused, be very careful removing the
               backplate, so that the stationary seat does not hit the shaft and become
               damaged.


  5. Wear Ring Removal
     For pumps that have a suction spool, the suction wear ring can be removed by removing the
     suction spool from the volute and driving the wear ring out with a hammer and a piece of wood.
     Otherwise, the suction wear ring can be removed by drilling the ring longitudinally in three places to
     relieve compression and collapsing the three sections together. Use care not to drill into the volute.
     Better control is obtained if small pilot holes are first drilled and then enlarged to "cut" the ring.

Reassembly

  1. Wear Ring Installation
     The wear rings may be pressed in, or driven in using a mallet with a rubber or plastic head. If a
     metal hammer is all you have, use a block of wood between the hammer and wear ring.

               Do not use a metal hammer directly on the wear ring or you will damage it.


  2. Backplate Installation
     If this pump has a run dry reservoir, make sure a gasket is installed between the frame bracket and
     backplate. Install the backplate.

  3. Seal Installation
     Clean the seal parts, the sleeve, and the bore for the stationary seat in the backplate. Wipe the
     lapped seal faces perfectly clean with a lint-free cloth. Lightly oil the o-ring on the stationary seat
     and press the seat into the backplate. A piece of plastic pipe makes a good tool to assist with
     installation of the seat. Lubricate the sleeve and slide the rotating portion of the seal on the shaft

                                   Torsional Vibration
Torsional vibration is a periodic, reversing twist in a torque transmitting member such as a shaft. This
twisting is superimposed upon the rotation of the shaft. Any rotational system has at least one torsional
natural frequency, the frequency at which the shaft will twist back and forth if a torque is instantaneously
applied then released. Internal combustion engines deliver torque in a periodic or pulsing manner and
consequently are very effective exciters of torsional vibration. If an engine/pump set operates too near a
critical speed, severe damage to the pump, engine, or related components can result. This is not
indicative of a defect in any of the components, rather it is a characteristic of any rotational system.

For pump/engine sets assembled by others, Cornell assumes no responsibility for torsional vibration
problems. This policy is consistent with ISO standard 3046/V-1978(E), applicable sections excerpted
verbatim below.

                      The supplier of a set may be the engine manufacturer or the manufacturer of the
                      driven machinery or a third contractor. If a purchaser buys an engine from one

                      manufacturer and the driven machinery from another, the purchaser is regarded as
                      the supplier of the set to himself.

                  Start-up and Operation Instructions
                                          Redi-Prime®

Pre-start Checklist

  1. Make certain that all belts and shaft couplings are properly adjusted and aligned and all guards are
     in place.
  2. Piping must be properly supported. Pump suction and discharge flanges must not carry pipe loads
     as resulting deflection can cause premature wear and failure of bearings, shaft, impeller, and wear
     rings.
  3. All electrical connections and equipment should be installed by a qualified licensed electrical
     contractor.
  4. Check oil levels in vacuum pump crank case and seal oil reservoir and add oil as necessary.
                 CAUTION: Damage will result if these oil levels are not properly maintained.


  5. Drain all fluid from stop cock (196) at bottom of diaphragm on vacuum pump. Be sure to close stop
     cock after all fluid is drained.
  6. Trailer and skid mounted units must be set on firm, level ground and wheels chocked.
  7. Ensure that hoses are correctly coupled and that the suction strainer is fully submerged.
  8. Ensure that all drain valves or plugs are closed.
  9. For indoor installations, make certain the air pump exhaust line to outside is in place and leak free.
               WARNING: For pumpages emitting noxious gases, the vacuum pump must be
               exhausted outside the pump house to avoid asphyxiation or explosion hazard.


 10. In freezing conditions, ensure that separator float movement is not impeded by any ice in the
     separator float box.

Operation

  1. Priming time is a function of the volume of air in the suction line and the RPM of the vacuum pump.
     High lifts and larger suction line diameter will require longer priming times.
                  CAUTION: Do not operate at higher than recommended speed to shorten
                   priming time or the service life of the vacuum pump will be shortened.


  2. If pump will not prime or maintain prime, or if vacuum pump runs hot or passes water, refer to the
     "Troubleshooting Guide" section in this manual.
  3. When pumping in extremely cold conditions, ice may form on the prime valve seat or linkage,
     preventing closure and allowing water into the vacuum pump. Close observation must be
     maintained and the pump shut down at the first signs of excessive water coming from the vacuum
     pump.
  4. Should the discharge check valve become plugged, the pump must be shut down and the valve
     cleaned out per the "Discharge Check Valve Operation and Servicing" section in this manual.
  5. Clogging of the suction strainer or hose may result in loss of prime without recovery. A vacuum
     gauge can be connected to the tap on the side of the separator float box to aid in diagnosing such
     clogs.

Dismantling (see pump manual for illustration and part numbers)

  1. Loosen clamp and disconnect air hose from priming valve.

  2. Drain the oil from the seal oil reservoir via the line on the lower reservoir connection. Disconnect
     the lower oil line at the reservoir, hold the disconnected end over a container and move the line and
     container down to below the connected end of the oil line. The is will drain the remaining oil from
     the lines and gland.

  3. Disconnect the oil lines from the gland and unbolt the gland only if further pump disassembly is
     planned.

  4. Remove the bolts and nuts holding the two halves of the vacuum pump belt guard together.
     Remove the two bolts and nuts holding the guard halves to the vacuum pump support table.
     Remove the belt guard halves.

  5. Loosen (do not remove) the four bolts securing the vacuum pump to the support table. Current
     design tensions belt by means of shims under the vacuum pump. Loosen support table bolts and
     insert large screwdriver or pry bar between vacuum pump and support table. Lift vacuum pump
     enough so that shims can be removed. Lower vacuum pump and there should now be sufficient
     slack to remove belt. Once the belt is removed, the vacuum pump can be completely unbolted and
     removed if desired.

  6. For pumps not equipped with and SAE adapter bracket for direct mounting to engine belt housing:
         l Remove the two Allen screws from the taper lock bushing on the main pump shaft pulley and

           insert one of the screws into the jack screw hole. Tighten to jack the pulley free of the taper
           lock bushing. The pulley and bushing can now be slipped off the shaft.
         l If the bearing frame is equipped with an SAE engine mount adapter for direct coupling to an

           engine belt housing, the engine drive coupling and SAE adapter will have to be removed
           before the main pump shaft pulley can be removed. Once the SAE adapter has been
           removed, the SAE adapter support bracket will be accessible. With the belt already removed
           from the vacuum pump shaft pulley, free the belt from the main shaft pulley and remove it
           from the support bracket. Now the pulley taper lock bushing can be loosened as per
           instructions above for non-SAE pumps, and the pulley can be slipped off the pump shaft.

  7. The vacuum pump support table and seal oil reservoir can now be unbolted and removed if
     desired.

Reassembly

  1. If the main pump bearing frame is to be serviced, refer to pump manual.

  2. For service of priming system components, refer to pump manual.

  3. Reinstall vacuum pump support table and reservoir.

  4. Orient oil reservoir so that inlet and outlet ports are on the same side of the pump as the inlet and
     outlet ports of the gland.

 5. Reinstall oil lines between gland and reservoir. The lower ports of the gland and reservoir connect
    together, as do the upper ports of the gland and reservoir.

 6. Reinstall pulley on pump main shaft. If pump is equipped with an SAE adapter bracket, position the

Float Valve Linkage Removal and Installation
There is normally no reason to remove or replace the float valve linkage unless parts have experienced
damage. In the event of required replacement, use the following instructions:
   1. Separate the linkage frame (RP10) from the cover (RP06) by removal of two 5/16" capscrews.
      There may also be stainless flat washers between the frame and cover. (It is important to replace
      these washers when replacing the frame, as the total mounting distance between the top of the
      cover and the frame is an essential element in proper priming valve operation.)
   2. All elements of the linkage are held in position with clevis pins (RP05), side links (RP07) and cotter
      pins (RP08). To remove any damaged element, straighten and remove the necessary cotter pins
      and clevis pins. NOTE: The two actuator arms (RP11 & RP12) are very similar in appearance.
      Before removing either of these parts, make special note of their position and direction in
      relationship to other assembly parts.
   3. Valve stem assembly (RP02) may be removed by a similar action of pin removal. Two cross drilled
      holes in the lower end of this part, with slightly separated center lines, accommodate accumulated
      tolerances in the assembled linkage parts. In most instances the drill from which the clevis pin has
      been removed will be one to re-use on reassembly.

Linkage Replacement
   1. Reverse the previous procedure steps.
   2. When the linkage is correctly reassembled and fastened to the cover, the valve seat o-ring (RP03)
      should seat firmly when the float ball is raised to its uppermost position, and a 1/8" minimum gap
      should remain between the two actuator arms (RP11 & RP12). If the arms can come together, the
      valve may not be adequately seating.

Strainer / Baffle
At factory assembly, the strainer (RP20) is pushed onto the bottom cone of the separator float box (RP15)
and is held in place by friction. During normal operation this strainer can drop loose from the cone and
rest on the slotted suction spool pipe. This is NOT a problem. Air being evacuated from the suction piping
can travel freely through the slots and around the strainer.

Maintenance
There is no regular maintenance to be performed on the separator. However, there are precautions for
operation during freezing conditions. These precautions are covered in the "Start-up Instructions /
Operation"section of this manual, and are repeated here for convenience:

In freezing conditions, ensure that there is no ice in the separator chamber to impede separator float
movement. Warm water can be poured over the separator, or into the separator chamber through the
separator hose connection. When pumping in extremely cold conditions, ice may form on the separator
valve seat preventing closure and allowing water into the air pump. Close observation must be
maintained and the pump shut down at the first signs of water coming from the vacuum pump.

NOTE: During an initial re-prime, there may be liquid remaining in the suction spool above the slots. This
may allow a brief misting out the discharge before the prime valve has closed. If at any other time during
operation, pumpage is seen continuously exhausting from the vacuum pump discharge, the pump should
immediately be shut down and the float box cover should be removed. Inspect the float assembly for any
impediment to its movement. It should be able to fully close the valve stem assembly to the valve seat.
Also check the prime valve for any damage or debris.

       Discharge Check Valve Operation and Servicing
The discharge check valve enables automatic priming of the pump by closing the air leakage path on the
discharge side of the pump. This allows the primer to pull the pump casing vacuum down sufficiently that
atmospheric pressure will push liquid into the pump. Once primed, the pump generates enough pressure
to push the check valve open and allow the flow of liquid through the discharge.

The presence of the check valve, as with any other fitting in the piping system, adds resistance (pipe
friction) to the total head the pump must overcome during normal operation, and not just while priming for
start-up. The amount of this added friction varies with the rate of flow, as well as with pipe size. To
determine the extent of the addition for a specific performance point, see appendix for chart.

If the check valve disc should be prevented from completely closing due to debris or damage, the pump
will not re-prime. In such a case it will be necessary to remove the inspection cover and clean out the
debris or service the valve. See the end of this manual for parts drawing and list.

Maintenance
Check valve requires no scheduled lubrication or maintenance. For service or inspection, the valve can
be serviced without removal from the line.

Valve inspection - if inspection of the valve is required, follow the disassembly instructions.

Disassembly
The valve can be disassembled without removing it from the pump.

                              Winterizing and Storage
                             Cornell Redi-Prime® Pumps

Storage Preparation
  1. Drain all water from main pump piping, including suction line and discharge line. Remove volute
     drain plug and vent plug to empty pump casing.
  2. Drain all water from stop cock (196) at bottom of diaphragm in vacuum pump. Be sure to close
     upon draining all water.
  3. Verify correct oil level in vacuum pump bearing housing and seal oil reservoir. Add oil as
     necessary.
  4. Add grease to main pump bearing frame fittings and vacuum pump pedestal fitting. Three to six
     strokes at each fitting with a hand grease gun should be sufficient.
  5. Jog the main pump motor for 10 to 15 seconds. Shut down motor and replace all plugs removed for
     draining.

Winter Storage
  6. Occasional motor starts once every month are recommended, with running for 10 to 15 seconds to
     replace and maintain a lubricating film on the bearings and operating parts.

Restart Preparation
  7. In installations where winter shut-down and storage is the normal situation, it is recommended that
     lubricating oil and seal barrier oil be drained and replaced to correct levels before the beginning of
     each new season.
  8. Add fresh grease to main bearing frame and vacuum pump pedestal fittings.
  9. Check all gasketed flanges, especially those on the suction side of the pump, for bolt tightness to
     eliminate the effect of air leaks on the priming time.
 10. Open stop cock (196) at bottom of diaphragm in vacuum pump to be sure all water and moisture
     has been drained. Close stop cock upon completion.
